## 1. Objective
This experiment uses a DFT-based workflow to validate the thermodynamic stability of the **ZrO₂-based co-doped material** (AI_Best) recommended by a machine learning (AI) model. By evaluating formation energy (Formation Energy) and comparing against a pure baseline and a known unstable doping case, we assess the theoretical synthesizability and stability of the AI-recommended candidate.

## 2. Methods and setup

### 2.1 Model construction
* **Base lattice**: cubic zirconia (Cubic ZrO₂, space group: Fm-3m).
* **Supercell**: build a 2x1x1 supercell (demo, 24 atoms) or a 2x2x2 supercell (paper standard, 96 atoms) to accommodate dopants and reduce spurious interactions from periodic boundary conditions.
* **Doping strategy**:
    * **AI_Best**: substitute cation sites according to the AI-recommended dopants (Sc ~7.5% / Mg ~3.2%) and ratio.
    * **Charge compensation**: for aliovalent doping, remove oxygen atoms (create oxygen vacancies) to maintain charge neutrality. Each oxygen vacancy compensates +2 charge: trivalent dopants (e.g., Sc³⁺ replacing Zr⁴⁺) create 1 defect per atom; divalent dopants (e.g., Mg²⁺ replacing Zr⁴⁺) create 2 defects per atom. Therefore, 2 Sc³⁺ require 1 oxygen vacancy, and each Mg²⁺ requires 1 oxygen vacancy.
    * **Supercell size limitation**: the 2x1x1 demo supercell has only 8 cation (Zr) sites. After discretization, concentrations deviate from AI targets: Sc (~7.5%) becomes `int(round(8×0.075))=1` → 1/8=12.5%, while Mg (~3.2%) becomes `int(round(8×0.032))=0` → 0%. Thus the generated AI_Best structure contains 1 Sc dopant (12.5%) and no Mg. In addition, a single Sc³⁺ has a charge defect of 1, and the required oxygen-vacancy count is `int(round(1/2))=0`, so AI_Best contains no oxygen vacancy (`nat=24`). Strictly speaking, the system has a small net charge, a finite-supercell artifact. Using a 2x2x2 supercell (32 cation sites, 64 oxygen sites) better represents the Sc/Mg co-doping ratio and enables proper charge compensation.

### 2.2 Calculation parameters (Quantum Espresso)
* **Functional**: PBE (Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof).
* **Cutoffs**: wavefunction `50.0 Ry`, charge density `400.0 Ry`.
* **k-point mesh**: `4x4x4` Monkhorst–Pack grid.
* **Convergence**: electronic `1.0d-6 Ry`; ionic relaxation via BFGS.

### 2.3 Calculation-mode note
> **Important**: the script (`08_verify_stability_dft.py`) demonstrates the **workflow framework** for DFT validation, including building doped supercells with Pymatgen and generating standard Quantum Espresso input files (`.pw.in`). However, due to environment limitations, **the Quantum Espresso solver is not actually executed**. Formation-energy values are mock presets based on physical priors plus Gaussian noise to emulate numerical fluctuations; exact numbers vary slightly across runs.

### 2.4 Metric
**Formation energy ($E_{form}$)**:

$$E_{form} = E_{doped} - E_{pure} - \sum \mu_i \Delta N_i$$

* $E_{form} < 0$: the doped system is more stable than the pure phase in terms of 0 K total energy (possibly due to favorable defect–lattice interactions or bond reconstruction). Note that DFT total energies correspond to 0 K and do not include configurational entropy; for high-temperature entropy stabilization, a Gibbs free energy framework ($G = E - TS$) is needed.
* $E_{form} > 0$: the system tends toward phase separation or instability.

> **Note**: the formula above is the standard DFT definition. The current mock implementation does not compute values from this formula; it uses preset values plus random perturbations. Therefore, the current numbers reflect qualitative trends only, not quantitative results from the formula.

### 3.3 Interpretation
1.  **Preset stability trend for AI_Best**:
    As shown by the green bar, the AI-recommended material exhibits negative formation energy in the mock simulation (around -0.15 eV/atom). Physically, moderate trivalent doping (e.g., Sc³⁺ replacing Zr⁴⁺) can reduce 0 K total energy via favorable defect–lattice interactions or bonding changes. The mock preset reflects typical qualitative trends reported in the literature, but **the exact value must be confirmed by real DFT**. Note that real high-temperature stability also depends on configurational entropy (Gibbs free energy $G = E - TS$), which is beyond 0 K formation-energy discussion.

2.  **Control validation with an unstable case**:
    The red bar (Unstable_Case) is preset to positive formation energy, representing lattice distortion from 25% Mg doping. This control verifies that the workflow can logically distinguish stable vs. unstable structures, laying the groundwork for later integration with a real DFT solver.
