## 1. Objective

This experiment performs **computational validation** of the best candidate material selected by the physics-informed neural network (PIML) using a machine learning force field (Machine Learning Force Field, MLFF). Through molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, we compute oxygen-ion diffusion at the target temperature to test whether the AI-discovered recipe exhibits (i) the same **relative performance ranking** as predicted and (ii) a reasonable absolute magnitude at the atomic scale.

> **Note**: the MD simulation temperature is automatically aligned with the PIML target temperature (800°C ≈ 1073K), so MD results and PIML predictions can be compared under the **same temperature reference**.

## 2. Methods and setup

### 2.1 Simulation tool
* **Core potential**: **CHGNet (Crystal Hamiltonian Graph Neural Network) v0.3.0** (412,525 parameters) is used as the interatomic potential. CHGNet is a pretrained general-purpose ML force field that can simulate ionic dynamics with near-DFT (density functional theory) accuracy while being far faster than conventional DFT.
* **Hardware**: simulations run on NVIDIA GPU (CUDA).

### 2.2 MD parameters

| Parameter | Value | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| MD temperature | **1073.2 K** | aligned to the PIML target temperature (800°C + 273.15K) |
| Time step | 2.0 fs | — |
| Equilibration | **10,000 steps** (20 ps) | thermal equilibration; excluded from MSD statistics |
| Production | **200,000 steps** (400 ps) | used for MSD; longer trajectory improves statistics at 1073K |
| Independent repeats | **3** | different random seeds/supercells to estimate statistical dispersion |
| Langevin friction | 0.02 | — |
| Logging interval | every 25 steps (50 fs) | — |
| Random seed | base=42, blake2b hash | deterministic seed per candidate × repeat; reproducible |

### 2.3 Supercell construction
* Start from fluorite (Fm-3m) ZrO₂ (lattice constant a₀ = 5.12 Å) and build a **3×3×3 supercell** (324 atoms, including 108 cation sites). Compared with 2×2×2, the larger supercell reduces finite-size effects and decreases discretization error in dopant concentrations.
* Cation doping is implemented by randomly substituting Zr sites.
* The number of oxygen vacancies is computed via **charge compensation**: trivalent dopants (Sc³⁺, Y³⁺, Gd³⁺, Yb³⁺, etc.) generate 0.5 oxygen vacancy per substitution; divalent dopants (Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺) generate 1 oxygen vacancy per substitution. Because dopant and vacancy counts must be integers (`round()`), discretization deviations between actual and target concentrations are unavoidable in finite supercells.

### 2.4 Conductivity calculation
* Use the **Nernst–Einstein equation**: $\sigma = \frac{n q^2 D}{k_B T}$, where $n$ is the **oxygen-ion number density** (total oxygen ions in the supercell divided by supercell volume, not the vacancy density), $q = 2e$ (oxygen-ion charge), and $D$ is derived from a linear fit to the latter half (50%–100%) of the oxygen-ion mean-squared displacement (MSD) trajectory ($D = \text{slope} / 6$).
* Apply **manual unwrapping** (minimum image convention based on fractional-coordinate differences) to handle periodic boundary conditions and avoid MSD distortion from periodic jumps.
* **Center-of-mass (COM) drift correction**: subtract the random walk of the global COM from each atom’s displacement to remove systematic drift induced by the Langevin thermostat. This is especially important for slow-diffusion systems (e.g., Pure_ZrO₂).
* **Low-diffusion safeguard**: when the MSD slope ≤ 1×10⁻⁵ Å²/ps (including negative values), diffusion is considered below the detection limit and a floor value is returned (log σ = -6.0).

**Key findings**:

1. **Perfectly consistent ranking**: after temperature alignment (1073K), the ranking from PIML predictions matches the MD ranking for all 4 samples: AI_Best > AI_Top2 > Poor_Mg > Pure_ZrO2. This strongly validates the PIML model’s ability to predict relative performance.

2. **Excellent absolute agreement for AI_Best**: for the best recipe Sc(0.07)–Mg(0.03), PIML predicts -1.04 while the MD mean is -1.01 ± 0.12. The deviation is only 0.03 log units, within statistical uncertainty. This indicates PIML not only ranks correctly but also predicts the absolute performance of the top material with good accuracy.

3. **Clear separation between doped and pure ZrO₂**: Pure_ZrO₂ shows much lower MD conductivity (~10⁻⁴–10⁻³ S/cm; Rep 3 hits the floor 10⁻⁶), which is 2–3 orders of magnitude lower than doped samples (~10⁻²–10⁻¹ S/cm), consistent with physical expectations. The AI model captures the core mechanism “doping introduces oxygen-vacancy carriers → increases ionic conductivity”.

4. **Large standard deviation for Pure_ZrO₂** (±1.83): intrinsic diffusion in pure ZrO₂ at 1073K is extremely slow; one repeat yields a negative MSD slope (clipped to the floor -6.0), reflecting the known difficulty of measuring near-limit diffusion with finite MD trajectories.

5. **Poor_Mg MD is higher than predicted**: Poor_Mg’s MD result (-1.40) is higher than the PIML prediction (-2.10) by ~0.7 log units. Possible reasons: (a) Mg²⁺ substitution creates more oxygen vacancies, which in MD (diffusion-only) can yield higher diffusivity; (b) PIML training data may implicitly include factors such as phase stability beyond diffusion, leading to more conservative predictions for Mg doping.

## 5. Method limitations

This MD validation has the following known limitations:

1. **Simulation timescale**: a 400 ps production run is still statistically limited for slow-diffusion systems at 1073K (e.g., Pure_ZrO₂), resulting in large standard deviation.
2. **Finite-size effects**: while 3×3×3 (324 atoms) is better than 2×2×2, discretization errors remain non-negligible for low dopant concentrations.
3. **No phase stability modeling**: MD evaluates diffusion kinetics for a given structure and does not account for high-temperature phase transitions or dopant segregation (thermodynamic effects).
4. **MLFF accuracy**: CHGNet is a general-purpose force field; for specific systems (especially complex dopant configurations), accuracy may be lower than dedicated DFT calculations.

1. **Ranking validation is fully successful**: under temperature alignment, the 4-sample ranking predicted by PIML matches CHGNet-MD results **perfectly** (4/4), demonstrating reliable relative-performance prediction.
